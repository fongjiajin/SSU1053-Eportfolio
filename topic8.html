LU 8 / Week 8

Organizational Communication

Reflection · SSU1053 e-Portfolio
01

Depth of Understanding

LU8 introduced me to organizational communication — the ways in which groups of people maintain structure and order through their symbolic interactions and allow individuals the freedom to accomplish their goals. The lecture explained that this structure is formed and maintained through structuration: the continuous process of creating norms and rules through verbal and nonverbal communication.

The lecture then broke organizations down into four types based on their orientation: Economic (producing goods, providing services), Political (generating and distributing power), Integration (mediating discord), and Pattern Maintenance (promoting cultural and educational regularity). Within these organizations, two central features stood out. First, the types of structures: traditional bureaucratic structures (with clear division of labor and a chain of command) and participatory structures (which value individuals' goals and encourage quality circles or autonomous work groups). Second, the communication networks: formal networks that follow prescribed channels, and informal networks that emerge from natural social interaction.

Finally, I learned about organizational assimilation (how members learn the organizational culture) and behavioral characteristics of competent communicators in the workplace, such as immediacy (psychological closeness), supportiveness, strategic ambiguity, and interaction management. However, the lecture also highlighted the "dark side" of workplace communication — including aggressive communication, workplace violence, and sexual harassment — reminding me that not all interaction in organizations is healthy or productive.

02

My Reaction

Before LU8, I had a very simplistic view of organizations. I pictured a strict hierarchy where the boss gives orders and the employees follow them. The lecture completely changed this. I was particularly fascinated by the concept of informal communication networks. I always knew about office gossip or "the grapevine," but I never realized how vital it is for an organization's actual functioning. Now I see that formal charts only tell half the story — the real work often gets done through these informal, natural social interactions.

The concept of strategic ambiguity also caught my attention. In communication, we are usually taught to be clear and direct. But in the workplace, using purposeful ambiguity to allow multiple interpretations can be a smart management approach. It showed me that organizational communication is not just about "being nice"; it is also about being strategic and navigating complex power dynamics.

However, the section on the "dark side" of workplace communication was sobering. Learning about quid-pro-quo harassment and hostile work environments made me realize that power imbalances can severely poison communication. It made me more aware of the importance of supportiveness and immediacy — not just as soft skills, but as protective factors that build a healthy workplace culture.

03

Practice / Application

Since LU8, I have become much more observant of the communication dynamics in the organizations I belong to — like my university clubs and student societies.

First, I am actively practicing immediacy and supportiveness in my team interactions. In an organization, it is easy to treat people just as "resources." Now, I make an effort to show psychological closeness (immediacy) by using eye contact, nodding, and giving positive feedback. When someone makes a mistake, I try to be supportive rather than critical. I have noticed that when I am supportive, my team members are more willing to admit their weaknesses and collaborate honestly.

Second, I am paying attention to interaction management. This means being skilled at starting conversations, developing topics, and ending conversations gracefully. Previously, I would just jump straight into the work. Now, I spend a minute or two asking about their day or their other classes before steering the conversation to the agenda. It creates a sense of belonging and makes the formal work flow much smoother.

Finally, the "dark side" of communication has made me conscious of setting boundaries. I know now that I don't have to tolerate aggressive communication or unsolicited behavior just because someone has a title. If I ever experience or witness workplace harassment, I will use my knowledge of formal networks to report it to the right channels (like HR or a trusted supervisor).

04

Benefit for Daily Life / Future

Since I will soon be entering the workforce, this lecture is arguably one of the most practical ones in the entire course.

In my future career, understanding organizational structures will help me navigate the system. I know that I need to be aware of the formal chain of command (who reports to whom) but also pay close attention to the informal networks (who actually gets things done). I will also strive to be a competent communicator. By mastering immediacy, supportiveness, and interaction management, I can build a reputation as a reliable, approachable professional. This will open doors for mentorship and career advancement.

In my personal life, this lesson extends beyond the office. Any group I join — a volunteer organization, a community project, or even my own family — operates as a mini-organization with its own culture. I now understand the importance of organizational assimilation (learning the norms) and structuration (maintaining the group's values). I feel much more prepared to step into any new environment and contribute meaningfully without losing my own identity.
