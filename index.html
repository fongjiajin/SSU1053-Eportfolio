Introduction
What is Social Interaction?

Social interaction is the dynamic process through which individuals connect, exchange ideas, express emotions, and respond to one another within a shared social environment. It is not simply talking — it is an ongoing cycle of observation, interpretation, and adjustment, where each person reads the behaviour of others and modifies their own response accordingly.

These exchanges build the relationships that shape our lives, from the intimate bonds we share with family and close friends, to the more structured connections we form at school, in the workplace, and across wider social communities.

Understanding how social interaction works helps us see how individuals construct meaning, negotiate identities, and navigate the complex web of norms and expectations that hold societies together.

This course is designed to guide us through that complexity step by step. Each learning unit focuses on a different aspect of the process — from the basic model of communication and the way identity and perception shape every message, to the role of listening, conflict, and social media in modern life. Taken together, these ten units form a complete picture of how humans relate to one another.

My goal in this portfolio is not just to record what was taught, but to trace how my own understanding has changed — how I have become more aware of the messages I send, the assumptions I make, and the relationships I build every day.

About This Portfolio
My Learning Journey

Welcome to my e-Portfolio for SSU1053: Introduction to Social Interaction at Universiti Malaysia Sarawak (UNIMAS). This portfolio is my personal space to document, reflect on, and share the knowledge I have gained throughout this course.

Before taking this course, I saw social interaction as something ordinary — just the way people talk and behave around each other. Through ten weeks of lectures, discussions, and reflections, I began to see it as a highly structured, deeply meaningful process that shapes identity, culture, and society in ways I had never fully appreciated before.

Each learning unit revealed a different layer of this process: the theories behind communication, the contexts that shape it, the role of media, the complexity of perception, the power of verbal and nonverbal messages, the art of listening, the nuances of relationships, the dynamics of groups and organisations, and finally, conflict management and social media.

This portfolio is a record of that journey — honest, reflective, and personal.

Why It Matters
The Foundation of Connection

Social interaction shapes self-identity, cultural practices, and social roles. It is the foundation of every relationship we build — personal, academic, and professional. Through interaction, we learn language, develop emotional intelligence, negotiate meaning, and discover where we belong in the social world.

Positive interactions build teamwork, trust, and a sense of belonging. When interaction breaks down, it leads to misunderstanding, conflict, and isolation. That is why studying social interaction is not just an academic exercise — it is preparation for life.

In SSU1053, we explore how communication operates across different contexts: verbal and nonverbal messages, perception and identity, listening, interpersonal relationships, small group and organisational communication, conflict management, and social media. Each topic adds a new lens through which to understand both ourselves and the people around us.

The course also teaches us to notice the subtle things — the silence between words, the tone behind a sentence, the expectations we bring into every conversation. These are the details that determine whether a message lands or falls flat.

By reflecting on these topics, I hope to become a more aware, empathetic, and effective communicator — not only in my future career, but in every relationship I build.

What You Will Find Here
Ten Learning Units

This e-Portfolio contains ten weekly reflections, one for each learning unit covered in the course. Every reflection follows a consistent structure, addressing four key elements:

  1. Depth of Understanding — the theories, concepts, and strategies presented in the topic, explained in my own words.
  2. My Reaction — whether I already understood the topic or discovered something completely new, and how my perspective shifted.
  3. Practice & Application — how I can use what I learned, with concrete examples from my own life.
  4. Benefit for Daily Life & Future — how the knowledge gives me an advantage in my career, personal growth, and social relationships.

Reflecting on these experiences allows me to recognise my progress and trace how my understanding of social interaction has evolved over the semester. It also gives me a personal record that I can revisit whenever I need to remind myself of what I have learned.
